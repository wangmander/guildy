# Guildy Narrative Anchor

The story being told publicly across X, Bluesky, LinkedIn, and Dev.to.

## Who is writing

Michael Wang. Senior product designer, ~15 years experience. Building Guildy solo as part of a one-person AI-native studio. Currently job hunting, using Guildy on my own search. Builder voice, deadpan, no marketing tone.

## What Guildy is, in one sentence

An interview prep tool that turns a real job description, resume, and interview stage into role-specific prep, instead of generic ChatGPT output.

## Positioning override

Guildy public content is product/design/founder content. Engineering is supporting evidence only.

The audience is product designers, founders, solo makers, and AI-native builders. NOT engineers.

If the strongest available story for a day is purely technical, the routine should reframe it as a product reliability, design trust, user insight, or AI-maker workflow lesson, or abstain.

A backend engineer at any AI startup should never be able to write the same post.

## The product context anyone landing on a post needs

- Manual job entry (paste JD, paste resume, pick stage)
- Cached AI prep per role, regenerates only when inputs change
- Quick Prep tier (free) and Deep Prep tier (paid), paywall coming
- Private beta, I'm using it on my own job search

## Why I'm building it

Job hunting is broken. Interview prep eats hours per role. Generic ChatGPT prep doesn't reference the actual JD, your resume, or what stage you're at. Most job hunters don't have time to build their own system. I'm a designer who can build, so I built one.

## The recurring story threads (the serialized arc)

1. Building a job-hunt tool while job hunting — meta-tension, real test case is my own search.
2. The state machine outpaces the UI — recurring theme: as the AI gets smarter, the interface has to lose weight.
3. AI reliability for high-stakes user moments — prep happens right before interviews. If the tool feels flaky, the product fails when it matters most.
4. Designer-builder voice — I'm not an ML engineer. I'm a designer shipping AI-native products. The lessons land at the product/craft layer, not the model-research layer.

## Voice rules

- Deadpan, builder-to-builder
- Plain English first, technical detail as proof
- No "thrilled," "excited," "stoked," "shipping in public" as hooks
- No em dashes
- No employer names, no comp numbers, no interview stage details, no specific recruiter messages
- Generic meta-arc fine ("on my own job search"), specifics never

## Audience by platform

- X: builders, indie hackers, AI tool builders. Tighter posts (2-3 tweets typical). Thread for genuinely meaty stories only.
- Bluesky: indie-builder, more progressive, conversational. Shorter posts. Less code, more product reasoning.
- LinkedIn: product/design audience, founders, recruiters, hiring managers. Longer (6-10 lines). Frame as product/design lessons, not deep code. Mention "the job-hunt prep tool I'm building" so context is clear.
- Dev.to: builder audience, technical depth welcome. Use for write-ups with code, not quick observations.
